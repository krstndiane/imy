Privacy

Last updated: 10 October 2026

The short version. Your chats, photos and memory notes are only visible to your account. When imy writes a reply, the recent messages and any photo you share are sent to our AI provider (Groq) to produce it. We don't share them with anyone else, and deleting your account removes them from imy. imy is for people aged 18 and over.

What imy stores

When you use imy, this is kept on the server:

  • Your account: your email address and a scrambled version of your password. The password itself is never stored, so nobody — including whoever runs imy — can read it.
  • The people you set up: the name and nickname you give them, your answers to the interview questions, and the profile pictures you choose.
  • What you upload: screenshots and chat export files, and the messages imy reads out of them. The original files are kept so imy can read them again if it gets better at it.
  • Your conversations in imy: what you send, what the person replies, reactions, and the photos and files you share in a chat.
  • Short notes imy keeps from your conversations — things like a date you mentioned — so replies can refer back to them. You can read and clear these in the chat menu.
  • One cookie that keeps you signed in. That's the only cookie. There is no advertising and no tracking across other websites.
  • When your account was last active, and which days you used imy — so the people running it know how many people use it. Just dates, never what you did. Days older than about 13 months are deleted.
  • Visitor counts, without cookies: when you open the homepage or the app, imy counts the visit as a number for that day. To count each person once a day, it makes a code from your internet address, your browser's name and a secret that changes every day. The codes and the day's secret are deleted the next day, so visits can't be linked from one day to the next or traced back to you. If your browser sends "Do Not Track" or "Global Privacy Control", you aren't counted at all.
  • Your time zone (like Asia/Manila), as your browser reports it, with that count and on your account. It's the only hint of where you are: the people running imy see a map with one dot per time zone, placed on that zone's main city. Your internet address is never looked up to find your location.
  • Feedback you send from the app: your note, what it's about, the rating if you gave one, and which screen you were on. Your email is shown with it only if you tick "You can email me about this".
  • When you agreed to the Terms, and when you confirmed a chat was one you were part of.
  • A daily count of how many AI replies your account used — just the number, not the messages.
  • Password reset links, if you use "Forgot password?": only a scrambled version is kept, and each link stops working after 30 minutes or once it's used.

Who can see it

Only you, from your account. Every person you set up belongs to that account, and no other account can open them, read their messages or load their photos — not through the website, and not by guessing the address of a file.

The people running imy have an admin page. It shows how many people use imy, and a list of accounts with each one's email address, when it joined, when it was last active, how many people it has set up and on how many days it was used — numbers only — and a map of the time zones people use imy from. It never shows the names of the people you set up, your messages, your screenshots or your photos. It also shows the feedback you choose to send.

Whoever runs imy can technically reach the server, its database and its backups, as with any website. Your data is not sold, not used for advertising, and not shared with anyone apart from the AI provider below and the email service that delivers password-reset emails (which sees your email address and those emails, and nothing else).

The AI that writes the replies

imy can answer on its own from patterns in the messages you uploaded. When a reply needs real understanding, it asks an outside AI service — Groq, and optionally Google's Gemini if that's been set up. What gets sent is only what's needed for that one reply:

  • a description of how the person writes, and a sample of their real messages;
  • the last few messages of your conversation;
  • a photo you share in a chat, so the reply can be about the photo.

Your email address, your password and your uploaded screenshot images are never sent. Groq says that by default it doesn't keep what's sent to it; it may keep it for up to 30 days to investigate errors or abuse unless that's switched off, and it processes it in the United States (Groq: your data). If Gemini has been switched on by whoever runs this site, Google's terms for the plan in use apply.

Each account can use the AI a set number of times a day. After that, replies are written by imy itself from the messages you uploaded, without sending anything out.

Deleting things

  • One person: open their chat, then the menu → Delete. Their messages, screenshots and shared photos go with them.
  • One conversation: the chat menu → Clear chat.
  • What they remember: the chat menu → What they remember → Forget everything.
  • Everything: your account menu → Delete everything. This removes your account, every person, every message, every uploaded file, your feedback and your activity dates from the server. It cannot be undone.

Deleted means deleted — not hidden. Backups made before a deletion may still hold the data until they age out, which takes about a week.

The person you're copying

This matters more than anything else on this page. imy learns from conversations involving a real person who isn't here to agree to it.

  • Only upload chats you were actually part of.
  • Don't use imy to impersonate someone to other people, or to pass its replies off as really theirs.
  • imy writes in someone's style. It is not them, it doesn't know what they'd really say, and it should never be treated as their words.
  • If you are the person someone has copied and you want that removed, get in touch below and it will be deleted.

Age

imy is for people aged 18 and over, which you confirm when you make an account. The AI services it relies on set that limit in their own terms.

Looking after yourself

imy can be a kind thing — a way to keep a voice close when someone is far away. It can also make a loss harder to sit with. If talking to it starts to hurt more than it helps, that's a good reason to step away for a while, and to talk to someone you trust. In the Philippines, the National Center for Mental Health crisis line is 1553, free from landlines and most mobile networks.
